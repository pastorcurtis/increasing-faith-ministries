From the Desk of Pastor Curtis
September 2026 – Kingdom Harvest

Grace and peace to you, beloved ambassadors of the Kingdom. As we step into this season of harvest, I greet you not merely as a shepherd but as a fellow laborer who walks daily under the authority of the One who reigns over all creation.

The Scripture declares, “The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few; therefore pray earnestly to the Lord of the harvest to send out laborers into his harvest” (Matthew 9:37‑38, ESV). The field is not a distant future hope; it is the present reality of every marketplace, classroom, boardroom, and home where Christ’s rule is either advancing or being resisted. Our mission is to align every thought, word, and deed with the Kingdom’s agenda, letting the gospel be heard as the proclamation that Jesus is Lord over every sphere of life.

Consider the promise in “And he said to them, ‘Go into all the world and proclaim the gospel to the whole creation.’” (Mark 16:15, ESV). “Whole creation” is the litmus test of our obedience. It compels us to speak truth into politics, to model integrity in business, to nurture creativity that reflects God’s beauty, and to raise families that embody kingdom values. The harvest is plentiful because the Spirit has already broken the first fruits of redemption into every domain; our task is to gather those scattered grains into the feast of God’s reign.

Practical Kingdom living begins with intentional presence. Set aside a regular hour each week to engage with your sphere—whether it’s a lunch with a coworker, a study group with students, or a community art project. Speak the lordship of Christ into those conversations, not as a vague blessing but as the concrete authority He holds over decisions, policies, and passions. Let the Holy Spirit empower you to be the embassy of the Kingdom, offering not just comfort but transformation.

As we labor, remember that the King Himself goes before us. He is the ultimate harvester, and He equips us with the tools of love, truth, and power. Rise, therefore, with confidence that your work is never in vain.

May you taste the joy of seeing lives turned toward the King this month, and may the fields you sow bear abundant fruit for His glory.

In Kingdom service,

Pastor Curtis

Baptist Pastor Martyred in Myanmar by Military Dictatorship

Rev. William, a Baptist pastor in Chin State, and fellow Christian Nam Ceu were extrajudicially murdered on June 4 by the Burmese military dictatorship, according to the Burma Research Institute. The two were arrested together before their deaths. Their killing underscores the brutal reality faced by Christian leaders under oppressive regimes. (Source: International Christian Concern)
Kingdom Perspective: Their sacrifice reminds us that Jesus’ lordship extends to every oppressed heart, and the Church must stand in solidarity, proclaiming the Kingdom’s justice even when it confronts tyrants.

Kingdom Living

Kingdom Living – September: Kingdom Harvest

The Kingdom is already breaking into every sphere of our lives, and September is the season to reap what we have sown. Jesus commands, “…let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and give glory to your Father in heaven” (Matt 5:16). Let those good works be intentional, Kingdom‑marked actions in the places you inhabit daily.

1. At Home – Cultivate Kingdom Rhythm

  • Set a 15‑minute family “Harvest Hour” each evening to read a passage together, pray for one another’s workweek, and identify one tangible way each person can demonstrate Christ’s lordship in the household (e.g., honoring a spouse’s schedule, serving a child’s need).
  • Replace one evening TV show with a short, Kingdom‑focused discussion or a worship playlist that declares Jesus over your home.

2. At Work – Declare His Lordship Over Your Tasks

  • Write a brief “Kingdom Commitment” on a sticky note for your workstation: “I will serve my clients with integrity, viewing every transaction as an opportunity to reflect the King’s character.” Replace it weekly.
  • Schedule a 5‑minute prayer break before the day’s busiest meeting, asking the Holy Spirit to give you wisdom to speak truth and demonstrate servant leadership.

3. In Community – Harvest Relationships

  • Identify three neighbors you don’t know well; invite each for coffee or a walk, listening for ways the Kingdom can meet a concrete need (childcare, grocery help, encouragement).
  • Volunteer a single hour this month at a local food pantry or after‑school program, explicitly praying that your service points others to the King who provides.

4. In Relationships – Extend Kingdom Grace

  • Choose one strained relationship; write a handwritten note acknowledging the other’s value and asking God to heal the breach, then follow up with a simple act of kindness.

Implement these steps faithfully. As you sow Kingdom‑focused deeds in home, work, community, and relationships, the harvest of lives transformed for Jesus will begin to appear now.

Scripture of the Month

Matthew 9:37‑38 (NIV)
“Then he said to his disciples, ‘The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest to send workers into his harvest field.’”

The Lord’s declaration is a proclamation of His Kingdom’s present reality: the field of the world is ripe, the harvest—new hearts, renewed cultures, transformed institutions—is abundant. Yet the laborers are scarce. Jesus’ words are not a future promise; they are a call to action in the here‑and‑now. The Kingdom is breaking into every sphere—education, business, media—just as the field spreads across the nation. The harvest is not a passive waiting but an active, urgent mission. As mature disciples, we are called to be the workers the Lord sends, equipped by the Spirit, to plant seeds of justice, compassion, and truth in our workplaces, classrooms, and homes. The short passage reminds us that the Kingdom’s work is immediate, not postponed to a distant age. It invites each believer to assess where they are field workers: are we sowing, weeding, or merely watching?

Meditation:
In what specific sphere of society do you feel called to be a “worker” for the Lord’s harvest, and how can you begin to step into that role today?
