November 2026 – Kingdom Gratitude

Grace and peace to you, beloved ambassadors of the Kingdom. As we step into the season of Thanksgiving, I invite you to view this ancient discipline not merely as a cultural ritual, but as a sovereign act of worship that declares Jesus’ lordship over every corner of our lives.

The Kingdom is not a distant hope; it is a present reality that invades our daily experience. When we give thanks, we are proclaiming that the One who created the heavens and the earth is the source of every blessing, and that His reign already governs the “family, business, government, and arts” that shape our world.

Psalm 107:1 declares, “Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good; his love endures forever.” This is not a passive sentiment but a bold proclamation that God’s goodness is operative now, sustaining the Kingdom’s advance in the marketplace, the classroom, the courtroom, and the home.

1 Thessalonians 5:18 commands, “Give thanks in all circumstances; for this is God’s will for you in Christ Jesus.” The word “all” expands the scope of gratitude to every circumstance—whether profit or loss, promotion or layoff, praise or criticism. Each moment becomes a battlefield where we declare that Christ reigns, and our thankful posture is a strategic act that aligns our hearts with the Kingdom’s agenda.

How do we live this Kingdom gratitude?

  1. Speak Kingdom language at work. Begin meetings with a brief prayer of thanks, acknowledging God’s sovereignty over the project and the people involved.

  2. Model thankfulness in the family. Teach your children to name one specific way God has shown His lordship each day, cultivating a household that reflects the Kingdom’s reality.

  3. Influence culture through grateful creativity. Write a blog, record a video, or create art that celebrates God’s rule, inviting others to see the world through the lens of a grateful Kingdom citizen.

  4. Engage government and community. Thank civic leaders for opportunities to serve, and pray that their decisions be guided by the values of God’s Kingdom, not merely personal ambition.

Let this Thanksgiving be a disciplined act of Kingdom expansion. As you give thanks, you are not only honoring the Creator—you are actively participating in the inbreaking of His reign on earth.

May the Holy Spirit empower you to make gratitude a daily proclamation of Christ’s lordship, and may your life become a testimony that the Kingdom is already here, thriving in the ordinary moments of your day.

In the authority of the King, go forth and thank Him in all things.

Pastor Curtis

Kingdom Living

Kingdom Living – November: Kingdom Gratitude

The Kingdom is not a distant promise; it is the power of Jesus’ lordship breaking into every sphere of our lives today. As we give thanks for His reign, let our gratitude be expressed in concrete Kingdom‑building actions.

Scripture anchor: “Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good; his love endures forever.” Psalm 107:1 – gratitude is the fuel that powers our obedience to the King over all creation.

Action Steps for a Kingdom‑Centered November

  1. Home – Declare the Lord’s Lordship over the household. Each evening, gather your family for a five‑minute “Kingdom Prayer” where you thank Jesus for specific blessings (work, health, relationships) and verbally submit every decision, schedule, and conversation to His authority. Write the prayer on a whiteboard so it becomes a visible reminder of His reign.

  2. Work – Transform your workplace into a Kingdom embassy. Choose one colleague each week to “bless” by offering genuine encouragement, a helpful resource, or a prayer note. Record the impact in a journal, noting how gratitude for God’s provision sparked tangible service.

  3. Community – Let gratitude move you into public service. Volunteer for a local food‑bank or neighborhood clean‑up on the first Saturday after worship. While serving, share a brief testimony of how God’s goodness in your life compels you to give back, inviting others to experience Kingdom generosity.

  4. Relationships – Cultivate Kingdom gratitude in friendships. Send a handwritten “thank‑you” card to three people who have influenced your faith this month, explicitly naming the ways they reflect Christ’s love. Ask them how you might pray for them, turning gratitude into mutual intercession.

  5. Personal Discipline – Keep a “Gratitude Ledger.” Each day, list three specific ways you saw Jesus’ lordship at work. Review the ledger every Sunday, offering the ledger to God as a sacrificial offering of thanksgiving.

Implement these steps throughout November and watch how gratitude becomes the catalyst that expands the Kingdom in your home, workplace, neighborhood, and relationships. The Lord calls us to thank Him by living under His reign. Let this month be a testimony of that truth.

Scripture of the Month

Psalm 145:10‑13 (NIV)
“All your works praise you, Lord; your faithful people … Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and your dominion endures through all generations. The Lord is trustworthy in all he promises and faithful in his love. The Lord upholds all who fall and lifts up all who are bowed down.”

The psalmist lifts a voice of gratitude that is rooted in the reality of God’s reign. “Your kingdom is an everlasting kingdom” declares that the sovereign rule of Jesus—who shares the Father’s throne—already stretches over every nation, culture, and circumstance. This is not a distant future hope but a present, unshakable authority that sustains creation. The psalmist’s praise flows from the awareness that God’s dominion “endures through all generations,” meaning that every moment of our lives is under His lordship. As believers, our gratitude must mirror this truth: we thank Him not merely for personal blessings but for His reign that upholds the fallen and lifts the bowed. In daily life, this means recognizing the Kingdom’s hand in our workplaces, families, and communities, and responding with thank‑you‑prayers that acknowledge Jesus’ rule over every sphere we inhabit.

Meditation: How does acknowledging Jesus’ present reign over every aspect of your day transform the way you give thanks?
