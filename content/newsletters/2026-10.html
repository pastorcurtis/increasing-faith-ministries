From the Desk of Pastor Curtis
October 2026 – Kingdom Authority

Grace and peace to you, beloved ambassadors of the Kingdom. As we step into this season of Kingdom Authority, I greet you not merely as a shepherd but as a fellow servant who walks daily under the sovereign rule of our Lord Jesus Christ. The authority we claim is not a vague “spiritual confidence” but the very power Christ transferred to His Church when He said,

“All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you.” (Matthew 28:18‑20)

Christ’s proclamation is a present reality: every sphere—government, marketplace, academy, arts, family—already bears His reign. When we speak of walking in authority, we are speaking of exercising that delegated rule in the ordinary places where the world seeks its own king.

Consider the apostle Paul’s declaration to the Romans:

“For the kingdom of God is not a matter of eating and drinking, but of righteousness, peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.” (Romans 14:17)

Righteousness, peace, and joy are not abstract virtues; they are the manifestations of Christ’s rule in our lives. When a manager chooses integrity over profit, when a teacher cultivates truth‑seeking hearts, when a parent models servant‑leadership, the Kingdom advances. The Holy Spirit empowers us to act, not merely to believe.

Practical Kingdom Living

  1. Declare the Lord’s ownership over your workplace. Begin meetings with a brief prayer of submission to Christ’s lordship, inviting His wisdom to guide decisions.
  2. Exercise judicial authority in your family by establishing biblical principles for conflict resolution—mirroring the justice of the King.
  3. Influence culture by creating content (posts, videos, art) that points people to the reality of Jesus ruling over every circumstance, not just the “spiritual” ones.

Each act, however small, is a strategic foothold for the Kingdom. The enemy will attempt to diminish our confidence, but remember that the same authority that raised Christ from the dead now rests in us.

Charge: Rise, Kingdom citizens, and claim the authority given you. Let your words, your work, and your worship be unmistakable testimonies that Jesus reigns now. May the Holy Spirit embolden you to be a visible embassy of the Kingdom, shaping every sphere for His glory.

In the authority of the King,

Pastor Curtis

This unprecedented restriction, occurring in a region already facing intense persecution, raises concerns among both believers and non‑believers about the church’s posture toward hospitality and witness. Kingdom Living

Kingdom Living – October: Kingdom Authority

Jesus reigns over every domain, and His authority is meant to be exercised by us today. As mature disciples, we are called to manifest that rule in the places we inhabit—our homes, workplaces, neighborhoods, and relationships. Let the reality of the Kingdom shape every decision you make this month.

Scripture Anchor – “Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters.” Colossians 3:23 (NIV)

Action Steps

  1. Home – Declare Jesus’ Lordship over the household agenda. Each night, write a brief “Kingdom Calendar” that lists one family activity (prayer, Scripture sharing, service) and one personal habit (e.g., limiting screen time) that honors Christ’s order. Follow through and review weekly.

  2. Work – Exercise Kingdom authority in decision‑making. Before any meeting or project, pause and ask, “Does this reflect the justice, integrity, and generosity of the King?” Then, commit to speak a truth that aligns with that answer, even if it’s counter‑cultural. Document the outcome in a work journal to track how Kingdom‑aligned choices affect productivity and witness.

  3. Community – Be a visible ambassador. Choose one local initiative (a neighborhood clean‑up, a school mentorship program, or a city council hearing) and register as a volunteer or participant. Use the opportunity to model servant‑leadership, offering a brief testimony of how Christ’s authority shapes your involvement.

  4. Relationships – Extend Kingdom grace. Identify a strained relationship and intentionally extend forgiveness or reconciliation this month. Write a short, sincere note that references Christ’s love and your desire to see the relationship reflect the Kingdom’s unity.

  5. Personal Spiritual Discipline – Guard your authority. Set a daily 10‑minute “Authority Hour” where you read a passage about Christ’s reign, pray for boldness to claim it, and journal a specific way you will exercise that authority that day.

By committing to these concrete steps, you will experience the present power of the Kingdom breaking into every sphere of your life. Let October be the month you walk confidently in the authority given to you as a citizen of God’s reign.

Scripture of the Month

Matthew 25:34‑36 (NIV)
“Then the King will say to those on his right, ‘Come, you who are blessed — inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. — For I was hungry and you gave me something to eat, I was thirsty and you gave me a drink, I was a stranger and you welcomed me, — I was naked and you clothed me, I was sick and you visited me, I was in prison and you came to see me.’”

The scene in Matthew 25 is a vivid portrait of Jesus’ present reign. He is not a distant future judge; He is the King who already sits on the throne, evaluating the kingdom‑building work of his people in the very here‑and‑now. The “King” pronounces blessing not on abstract belief but on concrete acts that embody the Kingdom’s authority over every sphere of life—social, economic, and relational. Each act of hospitality, provision, and compassion is a declaration that Jesus’ lordship extends into the marketplace, the courtroom, the classroom, and the home. As mature disciples, we are called to exercise that same authority, turning everyday encounters into Kingdom‑advancing ministries. When we feed the hungry, we demonstrate that the King’s provision supersedes any worldly scarcity; when we welcome strangers, we proclaim that the Kingdom’s citizenship transcends ethnic and cultural boundaries. Thus, the passage exhorts us to live out the Kingdom now, letting Jesus’ reign be visible in our daily deeds.

Meditation: In what specific ways are you already exercising the King’s authority in your daily interactions, and where is He calling you to extend that Kingdom influence further?
